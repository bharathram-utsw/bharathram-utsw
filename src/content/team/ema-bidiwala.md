**Cognitive and Cognitive Neuroscience** — Ph.D., Texas Tech University (2025)
**Biotechnology** — M.S., Texas Tech University (2024)

Ema received her Ph.D. under the mentorship of Dr. Miranda Scolari. Her doctoral research examined whether individuals could detect basic visual features such as line orientation and motion direction, up to 60° in the visual far periphery. Concurrently, she earned an M.S. in Biotechnology under the mentorship of Dr. Chiquito Crasto. Her thesis explored far peripheral vision as a potential early biomarker for Alzheimer's disease.

Her current research interest is understanding how biological sex and environmental factors influence aging and disease at the molecular level. She is particularly interested in genetic and signaling pathways involved in ocular health, disease, and other tissues, including the heart and brain.
